What Is a Bourbon of the Month Club? A Collector's Guide to Subscriptions, VIP Programs, and Whether They're Worth It

A bourbon of the month club delivers curated bottles to your door on a schedule. Learn how subscriptions, distillery VIP programs, and group picks differ—and which is right for your cellar.

By Pour Picks · Published September 3, 2026

In brief

A bourbon of the month club is a subscription service that delivers one or more curated bottles to your door on a monthly or quarterly schedule. Programs range from retail subscription boxes (Flaviar, Taster's Club, PourMore) to distillery VIP clubs that offer early access to limited releases and private barrel picks.

A bourbon of the month club is exactly what it sounds like, and also a lot more complicated than it sounds. At the simple end, it’s a subscription that drops a curated bottle (or two) on your doorstep every month or quarter. At the serious collector end, it’s a distillery VIP program that gates early access to limited releases, barrel picks, and experimental series behind a membership. Knowing which type you’re looking at, and whether either one fits your cellar strategy, requires some unpacking.

What exactly is a bourbon of the month club, and how does it work?

A bourbon of the month club is a membership that regularly delivers a full-sized bottle (or several) to your door, usually monthly or quarterly. The basic mechanics are simple: you sign up, choose a tier, and shipments arrive automatically. These services curate bourbon for you, ranging from sample-sized tasting kits to full-sized bottles, with someone else doing the work of finding producers that may not be available in your local market.

What varies widely between clubs is the curation philosophy. Some require that every bottle in the selection has won a gold medal or higher from competitions like the San Francisco World Spirits Competition, the New York Wine and Spirits Competition, or the LA Spirits Awards, or has scored 90+ points from publications such as Whisky Advocate, Wine Enthusiast, or Whisky Bible. Others rely on editorial picks or rotating themes: finishing casks one month, high-rye mash bills the next. Neither approach is inherently better. It depends on what your cellar already has and how much discovery you want built into the experience.

How is a subscription box different from a distillery VIP club?

A question we hear often: collectors use these terms interchangeably, but they’re meaningfully different things.

A retail subscription box (Flaviar, Taster’s Club, PourMore, and similar) is operated by a third-party retailer or spirits platform. It sources bottles from multiple brands and distilleries, curates a selection, and ships directly to members. Most include tasting notes, distillery backstories, and cocktail recipes. Many also offer members priority access to limited-run barrel picks or rare releases.

A distillery VIP club is run by the distillery itself and limited to its own portfolio. A recent example: Remus Bourbon launched the Remus Bottle Club in March 2026, targeting collectors who want limited-edition, hard-to-find bottles delivered every quarter. Members also get early previews of upcoming releases and invitations to virtual tasting events with the production team.

The practical difference for a collector: a subscription box broadens your exposure across the category, while a distillery club deepens your relationship with one producer and often offers the most direct path to allocated or experimental bottles that never hit retail broadly.

The group private barrel format deserves special mention. A private barrel selection is the purchase of a single aging barrel that your group picks from a curated set of candidates. The distillery bottles it exclusively for you, and the yield varies by barrel, typically 150 to 250 bottles. If Eagle Rare retails for $30 and your barrel yields 200 bottles, you’re looking at $6,000 total. That’s a serious commitment, but it’s also one of the most direct ways to own something genuinely one-of-a-kind.

What do I actually receive in a typical shipment?

This one comes up a lot: people assume every club sends the same thing.

Content varies substantially by tier and platform. Entry-level tiers often deliver one full-sized 750ml bottle with a printed card or digital insert covering tasting notes, distillery background, and suggested food pairings. Premium tiers may include two or three bottles built around a theme: a distillery vertical, a finishing-cask comparison, or a proof ladder from 80 proof to barrel strength. Some clubs layer in production-process explainers, video content, or community forum access alongside the bottle itself.

The better clubs offer selections that span a range of flavors, ages, and distilling techniques, so each shipment teaches you something rather than just adding to the rack. If you’re an established collector who already owns most well-known expressions, be honest with yourself: does this club surface bottles you’d genuinely never find in your market, or is it packaging up readily available shelf whiskey at a convenience markup? That question separates real value from a pretty box.

Is it actually worth it for a collector with an established cellar?

Readers frequently ask whether subscription clubs add value once you’ve already spent years building a collection, or whether they’re better suited to beginners.

The honest answer: it depends on which gap you’re trying to fill. If your cellar is deep in Kentucky straight bourbons but thin on craft producers, heirloom grain expressions, or independently bottled releases, a well-curated subscription can surface bottles you’d never proactively seek out. Whiskey market trends in 2026 show consumers confronting real questions about the role of limited-edition bottlings and collector mindset in an increasingly crowded marketplace, which means a trusted curatorial voice has genuine utility even for experienced palates.

Where subscription boxes consistently underperform for collectors is in allocation and secondary-market value. The bottles that matter most to serious hunters, things like Buffalo Trace Antique Collection releases, Pappy, Wild Turkey Rare Breed Barrel Proof, do not flow through subscription services. Those require distillery-direct lottery entry, retailer relationships, or auction participation. A subscription club is a complement to your hunting strategy, not a replacement for it.

One practical consideration worth flagging: many clubs renew automatically at the end of the subscription period, which can lead to an awkward situation if you forget to cancel and end up spending twice as much as you intended. Always read the fine print on cancellation and pause policies before committing.

Can I legally have bourbon shipped to my home through a club?

Readers frequently ask about the legality of alcohol delivery, and rightly so.

An adult (21+) must be present to sign for the delivery, but the bigger variable is your state. Direct-to-consumer spirits shipping laws vary dramatically by jurisdiction, and many states prohibit it outright or limit it to wine only. Services like PourMore rely on a network of third-party retailers, vendors, distributors, and couriers; when you place an order, it goes to a licensed third party who fulfills it. This fulfillment-through-retailer structure is how most subscription clubs work around the patchwork of state alcohol laws. The club itself never takes title to the bottles; a licensed retailer in a permitting state processes the transaction. Before subscribing, confirm your state appears on the club’s eligibility list, and keep in mind this landscape shifts as state legislatures revisit DTC shipping rules.

How do I evaluate a club before committing?

Look for five things:

  1. Selection transparency. Does the club explain how bottles are chosen: competition medals, proof minimums, distillery relationships, or editorial discretion? Opaque curation is a red flag.
  2. Relevance to your existing cellar. If you can find every bottle the club describes at your local Total Beverage, the club is offering convenience, not discovery.
  3. Geographic eligibility. Confirm your state is fully supported before entering payment information.
  4. Pause and cancel policy. A legitimate club makes it easy to pause or cancel without burying the process in fine print. If a club makes cancellation difficult, treat that as a warning.
  5. Community and education value. The best clubs add something beyond the bottle: tasting events, distillery access, forum communities, or barrel pick participation. Priority access to limited-run barrel picks or rare releases can be genuinely useful for collectors building toward their first private barrel experience.

If you’re tracking multiple bottles arriving from different sources (subscriptions, distillery direct, secondary market pickups), keeping a bourbon inventory app current becomes more important, not less. A monthly delivery habit adds bottles faster than memory can track provenance, proof, and purchase price.

FAQs

Is a bourbon of the month club worth it for serious collectors? It depends on your goals. Subscription boxes are best for discovery: finding bottles you’d never seek out on your own. Distillery VIP clubs are better for collectors who want early access to limited releases or barrel picks. Neither replaces targeted hunting, but both can fill gaps in a cellar and surface overlooked expressions.

Can bourbon be legally shipped to my home through a club? It depends on your state. Direct-to-consumer alcohol shipping laws vary widely. Most subscription services fulfill orders through licensed third-party retailers and couriers, but some states prohibit or heavily restrict spirits shipments. Always confirm your state’s rules before subscribing, and note that an adult 21+ must sign for delivery.

What is the difference between a bourbon subscription box and a distillery VIP club? A subscription box (Flaviar, Taster’s Club, PourMore) is run by a third-party retailer and curates bottles from multiple brands. A distillery VIP club (Remus Bottle Club, Buffalo Trace Single Barrel Select) is run by the distillery itself and offers direct access to its own limited releases, barrel picks, or experimental series. The two are complementary, not interchangeable.

How much does a typical bourbon subscription cost per month? Entry-level retail subscription clubs typically run $40-$80 per shipment for one full-sized bottle. Premium or multi-bottle tiers can reach $100-$200+ per month. Distillery VIP programs vary widely: some are free to join with per-bottle pricing, others charge a quarterly membership fee plus the cost of allocated bottles.

What should a collector look for when evaluating a bourbon club? Look for transparency about how bottles are selected (award criteria, proof minimums, or distillery relationships), a clear cancellation or pause policy, geographic shipping eligibility, and whether the club surfaces bottles you genuinely can’t find locally. Clubs that duplicate your everyday shelf finds offer little value to an established collector.
