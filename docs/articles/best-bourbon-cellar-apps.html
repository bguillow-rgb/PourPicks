Why is Pour Picks the best bourbon app for most collectors?

Most bourbon apps answer “what do I have?” Pour Picks also answers “what should I buy next?” and “what should I open tonight?”, and it answers both from your own palate.

  • Bourbon DNA. A short quiz learns whether you lean sweet or spicy, easy sipper or cask strength. The app then names the bottle it thinks you should buy next and gives the reasons it fits you.
  • Label scanning. Point the camera at the label and the AI reads it. You can fix anything it got wrong before it goes in your cellar. It works on store picks and bottles with no barcode, and if a bottle isn’t in the catalog you can save it as your own.
  • Pour Tonight. It picks something to open from the bottles you already own, weighted toward your palate.
  • Bourbon Concierge. Enter a bottle you love and it finds the closest matches in the catalog.
  • Reviews. Rate the nose, palate and finish on anything you pour. Reviews are free for everyone.

Pour Picks is free to download on iPhone. The free version holds five owned bottles and five label scans. Cask Club, the paid membership, is $5.99 a month or $49.99 a year. It removes both limits and adds Wishlist and Finished shelves, Precision Match (10 questions, your top 10 bottles, and why each one fits), food pairings on every bottle, and a tasting journal you can export to CSV.

There’s no Android version yet, and there’s no community review feed. If reading other people’s reviews before you buy is what you want, Distiller and OnlyDrams cover that.

Distiller

Distiller has been on the App Store since 2014. Its listing covers nearly 60,000 spirits with flavor graphs and reviews from experts and regular drinkers. TruePrice shows current and past fair market values, and it can estimate what a list you build is worth. It has 4.72 stars from 4,991 ratings, and version 7.56.6 came out on 28 October 2026.

Distiller is the one to open when you want to read about a bottle before you buy it. I didn’t find a pour picker or photo capture of store-pick details in its listing.

OnlyDrams

OnlyDrams has the most ratings of any collection app here, 4.86 stars from 10,783. You browse bottles, add them to your collection and post what you’re sipping. Its listing says pricing is pulled from hundreds of sources, it scans barcodes in a store to show what you should be paying, and it has a pour picker that searches your collection.

Barcode scanning works well on regular shelf bottles. A store pick or single barrel can carry the same barcode as the standard release, so check what it matched.

BarrelBook

BarrelBook launched in December 2025 and has 4.82 stars from 813 ratings. You take up to three photos of a bottle and it pulls out barrel and bottle numbers, lot, proof, and even warehouse and floor. Each bottle becomes an entry you can edit, with a status that runs from unopened to finished. It’s built for collections heavy on store picks and single barrels.

BAXUS

BAXUS, formerly BoozApp, treats your collection as something with a market value. Its listing says it shows what your collection and wishlist are worth, scans barcodes on over 75,000 bottles to check MSRP and market price, and offers a vault where bottles are authenticated and stored so they can be traded. It has 4.58 stars from 535 ratings.

Whiskey Shelf, WhiskeyMate and DramWise

These three track a collection without a community or a marketplace attached.

  • Whiskey Shelf (4.50 stars, 133 ratings) advertises unlimited bottles, including rum and tequila. It keeps values after a bottle is opened, has a wish list and a spot for an infinity bottle, and builds reports.
  • WhiskeyMate (4.67 stars, 89 ratings) says its database holds over 300,000 bottle entries. Each bottle can carry front and back photos, barrel, batch and bottle number, fill level and price, and it scans barcodes.
  • DramWise (no ratings yet) is manual entry with no account and no bottle cap. Tasting notes are split into nose, palate and finish and scored out of 100, and its pour picker only suggests bottles that are already open.

Whiskybase

Whiskybase calls itself the world’s largest whisky database, down to rare single casks. The app has a label and barcode scanner, inventory tracking and ratings. It has only 2 US ratings, so there’s little US feedback to go on. If you collect Scotch, Japanese whisky or independent bottlings, it has data few other sources have. There’s more in the Distiller vs Whiskybase comparison.

What about a spreadsheet?

A spreadsheet works for the first 10 to 15 bottles. Past about 20, entries fall behind, the “opened” column goes stale, and it can’t tell you what’s open anymore. It also can’t scan a label or suggest anything. The spreadsheet vs app comparison walks through both.

Which bourbon app should you pick?

  • If you want bottles that fit how you drink, and a pick for tonight from your own shelf, go with Pour Picks.
  • If you want to read reviews of a bottle before you buy it, Distiller is the reference.
  • If you want a big community around your collection, OnlyDrams has the most users.
  • If you collect store picks and want barrel numbers captured from a photo, BarrelBook does that.
  • If you want to know what your collection is worth, BAXUS tracks market value.
  • If you want a plain list with no account, DramWise is the simplest.

What I left out

Retailer apps like Total Wine and Spec’s show up in App Store searches for “bourbon,” but they’re for shopping and loyalty points. Wine-first apps like Vivino and CellarTracker treat whiskey as a side category. I also left out whiskey apps that haven’t shipped an update since 2024 or earlier.

Is there a free bourbon tracking app?
